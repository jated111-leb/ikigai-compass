# The Daily Compass #008 — Meaning is the scarce resource

*Sunday, July 19, 2026 · ~18 minute read*

A Sunday issue about scarcity in an age of infinite text: Borges's Library made queryable, model weights given away while the customization layer charges rent, and the two human archetypes the AI economy is minting — the maniacs who master it and the populists who resent it. Meaning, judgment, and effort keep turning out to be the things that don't commoditize.

---

## ── THE BRIEFS ────────────────────────────────

### ◆ 1. The blank slate and the bubble

**What happened.** Thinking Machines released **Inkling** — a 975B-parameter, Apache-2.0 US open-weights model — and Tomasz Tunguz named the business model: [the Blank Slate strategy](https://tomtunguz.com/the-blank-slate-ai-strategy/). Like Slate Auto's $24,950 stripped-down pickup, you ship a deliberately plain, cheap base and monetize what people build on it: "the weights are free, but the customization charges rent." The same week, Moonshot's open-weight Kimi K3 triggered what traders called a new DeepSeek shock — TSMC down 7%, SoftBank down 9% on Friday.

**The excerpt** — Howard Marks, from his memo ["Is It a Bubble?"](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/is-it-a-bubble):

> "It's okay to supply debt financing for a venture where the outcome is uncertain. It's not okay where the outcome is purely a matter of conjecture. Those who understand the difference still have to make the distinction correctly."

**Why it matters.** Hold the two together and enjoy the arc: Marks's memo quotes an investor calling Thinking Machines' $2B no-product seed round "the most absurd pitch meeting" — and that same lab just shipped the largest open-weights model in American history, with a rent-collecting toolchain attached. Both things can be true: bubble-era behavior *and* a real business model being discovered live. Marks's deeper frame — separate the bubble in *company* behavior from the bubble in *investor* behavior, and watch the unprecedented use of debt in a winner-take-all race — is the right lens for a week when free intelligence cratered chip stocks. His bottom line is the usable one: nobody should be all-in, and nobody should be all-out.

*Sources: Tunguz (Jul 16); Marks, Oaktree (Dec 2025); Fortune on the Kimi shock (Jul 17). ~25 min.*

### ◆ 2. The maniacs and the populists

**What happened.** Two essays map the human poles of the AI economy. Tyler Cowen's Free Press column describes ["AI maniacs"](https://www.thefp.com/p/tyler-cowen-ai-maniacs-future-economy) — obsessives who master each new model and run one-person companies with agent swarms (Stripe: $10M+ solo firms doubled in two years); they skew young, immigrant, and credential-skeptical, "as they should be. If you wish to learn how to manipulate AI tools, Harvard and Yale are not the places to go." Jasmine Sun's ["AI Populism's Warning Shots"](https://jasmi.news/p/warning-shots) — written after shots were fired over a data center and Altman's home was attacked twice — describes the opposite pole: people for whom AI is not a technology to evaluate but an elite project to resist.

**The excerpt** (Sun):

> "From now until forever, most people regulating, protesting, and talking about AI will not be interested in AI *per se*, but rather how it impacts their preexisting belief systems and political agendas."

**Why it matters.** These are the same phenomenon seen from opposite ends: the surplus is flowing to self-taught obsession while the legitimacy is draining from institutions that can't explain who benefits. Sun's sharpest observation — caring about AI has fully decoupled from knowing about AI; "because AI" is the new "because China" — predicts the politics of every data-center fight and open-weights EO to come. Cowen predicts the labor market; Sun predicts the backlash; a strategy for the next decade has to price both. Her proposed resolution — a visible, redistributive grand bargain — is the part nobody in the maniac class is building yet.

*Sources: Cowen, The Free Press (mid-July); Sun (April). ~20 min.*

### ◆ 3. The Library made queryable

**What happened.** No news here — a pairing that this week finally earned. Borges's 1941 ["The Library of Babel"](https://hex.ooo/library/library_of_babel.html) imagined a universe containing every possible book: every truth, every refutation, drowned in gibberish — and traced what infinite text does to its librarians: euphoria ("all men felt themselves to be the masters of an intact and secret treasure"), then despair, sects, Purifiers. Ted Chiang's 2023 ["ChatGPT Is a Blurry JPEG of the Web"](https://www.newyorker.com/tech/annals-of-technology/chatgpt-is-a-blurry-jpeg-of-the-web) is the Library run in reverse: the model as lossy compression of everything written, hallucinations as compression artifacts.

**The excerpts** — Borges:

> "The certitude that everything has been written negates us or turns us into phantoms."

Chiang, on why the blur matters for anyone who writes:

> "Your first draft isn't an unoriginal idea expressed clearly; it's an original idea expressed poorly, and it is accompanied by your amorphous dissatisfaction, your awareness of the distance between what it says and what you want it to say."

**Why it matters.** Between them they state the epistemic condition of 2026: text is unlimited; meaning — verification, judgment, the dissatisfaction that drives revision — is the scarce resource. Chiang's aged-imperfectly prediction is itself instructive: he warned that models trained on model output would degrade like photocopies of photocopies, yet labs now train on synthetic text deliberately — the Xerox metaphor held, the engineering answer changed. For the applied version of "truth-finding as a discipline," this week's podcast queue has the founder of Bellingcat.

*Sources: Borges (1941); Chiang, The New Yorker (2023). ~30 min, worth every one.*

## ── RAPID FIRE ────────────────────────────────

**The frontier, compressed** — Anthropic made Fable 5 *permanently* available on Max/Team plans from July 20 (Willison's read: the access-uncertainty strategy finally reversed under GPT-5.6 and Kimi K3 pressure — the "colander" logic from issue #004 winning). And Apple sent litigation-hold letters to ~40 ex-employees now at OpenAI. ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/18/claude-make-fable-5-permanent/), Jul 18)

**Casual destruction** — 3QD's Jeroen Bouterse takes on the "Carl Schmitt's moment" discourse by resurrecting Richard Thoma, the forgotten Weimar jurist who defended parliamentary democracy against him: Schmitt "did exactly what illiberal propagandists are doing in our time: driving wedges between the popular will and democratic institutions." Study the defenders of flawed democracies, not just their most brilliant enemy. (Jeroen Bouterse, [3 Quarks Daily](https://3quarksdaily.com/3quarksdaily/2026/07/casual-destruction.html), Jul 17)

**Vienna's cafés, Belgrade's kafanas** — last issue's coda, now verified and worth a full slot: the same institution incubated aphoristic modernism in one city and the Sarajevo conspiracy in the other. Trotsky on the Viennese: they could not see "the gigantic soldier's boot over the ant-heap in which they were rushing about with such self-abandon." A study in how discourse spaces shape what their inhabitants cannot see. (Anton Cebalo, [Aeon](https://aeon.co/essays/viennas-cafes-inspired-poets-belgrades-bred-dissent), Jul 16)

**The air-conditioning culture war** — Musk trolls sweltering Europe with Lee Kuan Yew's line — "air conditioning… changed the nature of civilization by making development possible in the tropics" — and Noema's editor, who conducted that very interview in the 1990s, watches his own archive weaponized. AC adoption (25% of French homes vs 90% of American) as a proxy war between climate adapters and cultural traditionalists. (Nathan Gardels, [Noema](https://www.noemamag.com/the-heated-debate-in-europe-over-air-conditioning/), Jul 17)

**Iran's theory of victory** — outgunned but not outplayed: its "mosaic defense" survived decapitation, and "nineteen weeks ago, Iran faced the combined might of the most powerful country in the world… Today, it is dictating the terms of the peace." Whoever's theory of victory maps to the political layer, not the kinetic one, dictates terms — a frame that generalizes to any resource-inferior competitor. (Shuker & Milburn, [War on the Rocks](https://warontherocks.com/outgunned-but-not-outplayed-irans-theory-of-victory/), Jul 14)

**Governing the driverless and the ownerless** — a governance double: Tabarrok argues autonomous vehicles should be insured, not litigated (UK-style: insure the vehicle, pay victims fast, let insurers — "continuous safety regulators" — subrogate); and a new SSRN paper shows why AI-agent legal personhood misses the point — two millennia of nonhuman legal persons (corporations, idols, rivers) all worked through *locatable human officeholders*, which is exactly what agents lack. Registration regimes, not personhood debates. ([Marginal Revolution](https://marginalrevolution.com/), Jul 18; [Rajagopalan, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7127038))

**A game of tonnage** — Farnam Street's Sunday Brain Food, via Seinfeld: "No one's really that great… It's a game of tonnage." Plus Albert Gray's old line that successful people are driven by purpose, not preference — doing what failures won't. The anti-shortcut issue of the week. (Shane Parrish, [Brain Food #690](https://fs.blog/brain-food/july-19-2026/), Jul 19)

**⏳ TIMELESS: The Psychology of Human Misjudgment** — Munger's 25 causes of bad judgment, incentive-caused bias first: "I've always underestimated that power. Never a year passes but I get some surprise that pushes a little further my appreciation of incentive super-power." Why now, three ways: Buffett just warned of "a great increase in the number of people doing dumb things"; every AI-capex argument is an incentives argument; and the commonest counterparty in any trade is someone in the grip of one of these 25. (Charlie Munger, [via fs.blog](https://fs.blog/great-talks/psychology-human-misjudgment/), 1995)

**🃏 WILDCARD: Matrix Miriam and the Mystic Jewels** — 3QD's Bill Benzon answers "how do we stay human in the AI revolution" with fiction instead of a manifesto: a near-future story of musicians building a new AI architecture, set in Kim Stanley Robinson's *New York 2140* — "centered on the activities of *Homo ludens* rather than *Homo economicus*." Play as the survival strategy. (Bill Benzon, [3 Quarks Daily](https://3quarksdaily.com/3quarksdaily/2026/07/the-mystic-jewels-the-vatican-and-matrix-miriam.html), Jul 17)
