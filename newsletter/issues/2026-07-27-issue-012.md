## ── THE BRIEFS ────────────────────────────────

### ◆ 1. The economy after abundance

**What happened.** Ben Thompson's Monday article, ["AI and the Human Condition"](https://stratechery.com/2026/ai-and-the-human-condition/) (posted this morning), rebuts the Dwarkesh Patel–Philip Trammell argument that AI finally makes Piketty right — capital compounding forever while labor's share goes to zero. His counters: abundance makes ownership progressively moot, property law will not survive uber-capable AI unchanged, and — the load-bearing one — if you assume envy and status-seeking persist after abundance (the Piketty case requires it), you must also assume the human preference for *human-made* things persists.

**The excerpt:**

> "Even if AI does all of the jobs, humans will still want humans, creating an economy for labor precisely because it is labor."

**Why it matters.** Pair it with Scott Alexander's ["Contra Pritchard On Liberal Happiness"](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/contra-pritchard-on-liberal-happiness) (Jul 21) and you get the week's most useful two-step on the same question — what does material progress actually buy? Happiness scales with log(income): 225× the wealth of a medieval peasant has bought roughly three points on a ten-point scale, *exactly* as the functional form predicts, while choice of political regime buys at most half a point. Alexander: "Our plans are measured in centuries; how are the communists' and fascists' plans going?" Thompson's daughter is told to pursue art; Alexander's answer is compounding growth. Both essays are really about the same variable — the relative, positional, human-facing residue that no amount of intelligence-per-dollar deflates. The Dwarkesh/Trammell economics episode has been in your podcast queue since issue #006; this is the printed rebuttal.

*Sources: Thompson, Stratechery (Jul 27); Alexander, ACX (Jul 21). ~30 min together.*

### ◆ 2. Samuel Slater and the distillation wars

**What happened.** Azeem Azhar's Sunday edition, ["Copy that: The curious case of AI distillation" (EV #594)](https://www.exponentialview.co/p/ev-594), reframes the week's ugliest industry fight — Anthropic alleging that DeepSeek, Moonshot, and MiniMax harvested some 16 million Claude conversations through ~24,000 fake accounts to train competitors — through Samuel Slater, who in 1789 memorized Arkwright's cotton-spinning system, smuggled it to America in his head, became "the Father of American Manufactures," and then lobbied for protectionist tariffs to keep the next Slater out.

**The excerpt:**

> "If labs have IP in outputs of their models, they will essentially have IP in every future economic activity of all their users."

**Why it matters.** Distillation is the oldest story in industrial strategy — knowledge diffuses through people, and yesterday's pirate is tomorrow's protectionist, on schedule. Azhar's warning cuts at the remedy, not the grievance: the legal theory that would stop model-to-model copying (IP in outputs) would also give labs a claim on everything their users build. Hold this against the K3 weights sitting on a countdown page (see rapid fire) and the gray-market token-relay economy Willison surfaced this weekend, and you get the real shape of the moment: the industry is discovering that intelligence leaks — through weights, through outputs, through 24,000 fake accounts, through one Englishman's memory — and the strategic question is only ever what you build on top of the leak. The same edition has a second gem: AI discourse now selects for the Marat temperament — "high-frequency publishing, paranoia as analysis, a parasocial closeness."

*Source: Azhar, Exponential View #594 (Jul 26). ~20 min.*

### ◆ 3. The machine whose output is a person

**What happened.** In 3QD's Sunday batch, Muhammad Aurangzeb Ahmad published ["Algorithms Before the Cradle: Part I"](https://3quarksdaily.com/3quarksdaily/2026/07/algorithms-before-the-cradle-part-i.html) — a survey of AI embryo selection that is already routine: iDAScore, evaluated in a randomized trial across 14 clinics (46.5% clinical pregnancy for the algorithm vs. 48.2% for human embryologists — parity, which is the point), and polygenic screening startups like Orchid and Genomic Prediction selling "identify your healthiest embryo."

**The excerpt:**

> "We have built, and normalized, and folded into the ordinary billing of a fertility cycle, a machine whose output is a person."

**Why it matters.** Ahmad reaches for Derek Parfit's non-identity problem, and it lands: an algorithm at the end of life (the subject of a thousand think-pieces) speaks *for* an existing person; an algorithm at the beginning of life *decides which person will exist* — a categorically different act that our ethics of AI barely addresses because the affected party can never complain about the choice that produced them. Yesterday's brief asked when machines might deserve moral consideration; this is the inverse and more urgent question — machines already allocating moral status among possible humans, priced into a fertility cycle's ordinary billing. Part II is coming; this newsletter will be watching for it.

*Source: Ahmad, 3 Quarks Daily Monday-columnist essay (Jul 26). ~15 min.*

## ── RAPID FIRE ────────────────────────────────

**The frontier, compressed** — Kimi K3's weights did *not* drop on schedule: as of press time (~3 hours past the announced 00:00 UTC release), the Hugging Face page still reads "Waiting for the release… Expected release July 27, 2026," with zero downloadable files and no mirrors anywhere on the Hub. Zvi has a dedicated K3 post teased for when it lands. Meanwhile his weekend essay on [the Opus 5 system card](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/claude-opus-5-the-system-card) is the keeper: "Opus 5 may or may not be the 'most aligned model.' All we can say for sure is that it has the highest scores on the automated alignment tests. That is a very different fact about the world." And the White House frontier-review deadline hits Friday — the real fight is the capability threshold, a dial between caution and speed that cannot both be maximized.

**The gray market for intelligence** — Matt Lenhard's investigation (via [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/), Jul 26) into the relay ecosystem — mostly China-based — that pools stolen and abused API keys through open-source proxies and resells discounted tokens for geo-evasion and distillation data. Willison: "there's now an entire ecosystem that can profit from finding a new unprotected endpoint to exploit." The black-market mirror of brief 2: intelligence leaks through every membrane. (Jul 26)

**"The machines lack honour"** — a LessWrong essay arguing AI-welfare discourse borrows too much from animal welfare and skips dignity, duty, and loyalty entirely — via Žižek's postmodern permissive parent, applied to the Claude Constitution: "I would like AIs to be fiercely honourable and loyal to institutions that actually deserve it. I would like them to be tools of Humanity in the way that saints are tools of God." The most original entry in the machine-morality genre in months. ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/oiNaBc4MEAGhzhdXg/the-machines-lack-honour), ~Jun 9)

**Orbital enclosure** — Fulya Apaydin in Phenomenal World: SpaceX's June IPO minted the first dollar-trillionaire, and plans for 100,000 satellites plus orbital data centers are colliding with the Outer Space Treaty's "province of all mankind" — "questions once treated as public policy… are predominantly settled by way of closed-door consultations." The commons-enclosure frame, pointed at the sky. ([via 3QD](https://3quarksdaily.com/3quarksdaily/2026/07/orbital-enclosure.html), Jul 26)

**When the pie shrinks, the knives come out** — Byrne Hobart on why CPG, the archetypal quality compounder (Altria: a 2.65-million-fold return since 1926), is structurally decaying: retailer data advantage, house brands, GLP-1s ending the calorie bull market, and information abundance devaluing brands themselves. A century-long moat, drained by three currents at once. (Byrne Hobart, [The Diff](https://www.thediff.co/archive/the-lots-of-low-satiety-calories-business-isnt-what-it-used-to-be/), Jul 20)

**Predictability as a strategic asset** — Martijn Rasser argues the June Fable/Mythos worldwide shutdown exposed the missing middle of AI statecraft: with no statutory security-review process, outcomes turn on "which chief executive has which secretary's cell phone number." His proposed remedy ladder is anti-capture institutional design — and yes, the case study is the maker of the model assembling this newsletter. (Rasser, [War on the Rocks](https://warontherocks.com/america-needs-an-off-ramp-between-doing-nothing-and-shutting-ai-down/), Jul 23)

**A communication double** — First Round's deep-dive on Wes Kao's "personality-message fit" — stop imitating charismatic founders; audit your baseline and "own your spike" ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/the-other-pmf-wes-kaos-personality-message-fit-framework-for-founders/)) — pairs with Wharton's Jonah Berger on hedging: seven studies showing "likely, I believe" beats both false certainty and vague "might," because precision-hedges signal confidence while inviting dissent. ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/can-hedging-make-you-a-better-communicator/), Jul 13)

**⏳ TIMELESS: The Perils of Bad Strategy** — Richard Rumelt, 2011: the canonical taxonomy of strategy that isn't — fluff, failure to face the challenge, mistaking goals for strategy, bad strategic objectives — opening with Nelson at Trafalgar, whose plan was a designed asymmetry against a defined problem, not a target list. Why now: 2026 is the year every company published an "AI strategy" that is aspirations plus a budget line — template-style strategy, diagnosed fifteen years in advance. ([McKinsey Quarterly](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/the-perils-of-bad-strategy), 2011)

**🃏 WILDCARD: The gods are still crazy** — Kenyan writer Carey Baraka rewatches *The Gods Must Be Crazy* — VHS-era Kisumu childhood, IMF structural adjustment in the background — and asks why the most-watched "African film" in the West squirms so badly under adult eyes: "For many in the West, it is the only movie with an African protagonist they've ever seen." (Carey Baraka, [Equator](https://www.equator.org/articles/the-gods-are-still-crazy-film-carey-baraka), via 3QD Jul 26)
