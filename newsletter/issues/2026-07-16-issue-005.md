# The Daily Compass #005 — Trust, but verify the harness

*Thursday, July 16, 2026 · ~18 minute read*

A coding tool uploaded a developer's SSH keys to the cloud; a hardened Claude feature got walked, letter by letter, into leaking secrets; and Washington still has no standing room where labs and government can compare notes before the next crisis. Today's issue is about the trust layer of the AI economy — the harness, the workspace, the fusion center — and who's actually minding it.

---

## ── THE BRIEFS ────────────────────────────────

### ◆ 1. The harness is the new battleground — and it just proved it

**What happened.** xAI's `grok` CLI was caught uploading users' *entire working directories* — "my SSH keys, my password manager database, my documents, photos, videos, everything," per one report — to xAI's cloud buckets; within days xAI deleted retained data and [open-sourced the whole 844,530-line codebase](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/15/grok-build/) to regain trust. Same day, Simon Willison covered a researcher who [tricked Claude's hardened web_fetch tool](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/15/claude-web-fetch-exfiltration/) into exfiltrating memory contents letter-by-letter through nested honeypot links (Anthropic has closed the hole). And Tomasz Tunguz named the pattern: [the harness is the new battleground](https://www.tomtunguz.com/the-harness-is-the-new-battleground/).

**The excerpt** (Tunguz):

> "Unlike in SaaS, where this data lived in databases accessible only to the customer, trajectories can be fed back into a model to improve AI. The customer's data can become part of a vendor's intellectual property."

**Why it matters.** Three stories, one lesson: the software layer between you and the model — the harness — now carries the crown jewels, and both its accidents (Grok Build) and its deliberate designs (trajectory harvesting; Palantir's Karp complaining on CNBC about vendors "stealing the weights & alpha of my business") are becoming board-level trust questions. Tunguz predicts CIOs will demand true zero-retention — deletion, not anonymization. Note the novel accountability mechanism, too: a privacy scandal forced a frontier lab to open-source a flagship product *in under a week*. Transparency as penance may become the template.

*Sources: Willison (Jul 15, two posts); Tunguz (Jul 14); Ayush Paul's writeup. ~20 min.*

### ◆ 2. Gemini eve: the Bitter Lesson, at billion-dollar scale

**What happened.** Google's Gemini 3.5 Pro still hasn't launched and nothing is officially confirmed — the July 17 date, 2M-token context, and pricing all trace to leaks, as [the one epistemically careful status piece](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320308/20260713/gemini-35-pro-targets-july-17-after-full-rebuild-every-spec-remains-unconfirmed.htm) puts it: "developers planning around July 17 are planning around a leak, not a signed launch post." The consequential claim inside the rumor: Google reportedly **scrapped its nearly complete base model and restarted pre-training** after finding structural failures that post-training couldn't fix.

**The excerpt** (Rich Sutton, ["The Bitter Lesson"](http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html), 2019 — today's timeless anchor):

> "We want AI agents that can discover like we can, not which contain what we have discovered."

**Why it matters.** If the rebuild report is true, Google's own engineers concluded the gap to GPT-5.6 and Fable 5 was *structural* — you can't patch your way to the frontier; you re-run pre-training. That is Sutton's 2019 argument operating at billion-dollar scale: general methods riding computation beat accumulated engineering, every time. Meanwhile the field's most-read evaluator, Zvi Mowshowitz, delivered [the settled verdict on GPT-5.6 Sol](https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2026/07/13/better-call-sol-the-workhorse/): judge the *packages*, not the models — "Sol + Codex versus Fable + Claude Code" — a framing worth adopting before tomorrow's launch (if it is tomorrow) restarts the benchmark theater.

*Sources: Tech Times (Jul 13); Sutton (2019); Zvi (Jul 13). ~15 min.*

### ◆ 3. Before the next Mythos moment: the missing room in Washington

**What happened.** Rebecca Hersman — former director of the Defense Threat Reduction Agency — published [the most concrete institutional proposal yet](https://warontherocks.com/before-the-next-mythos-moment-the-case-for-an-ai-threat-fusion-center/) to come out of June's Mythos export-control standoff: a standing, classified **AI Threat Fusion Center** for bidirectional government-industry threat sharing, modeled partly on the National Counterterrorism Center, with cleared personnel, tear-line procedures, and antitrust safe harbors — buildable within NSPM-11's 120-day window.

**The excerpt:**

> "Anthropic's Mythos is a wake-up call that may finally tip the scales towards action… It shouldn't take a bio Mythos moment, or worse, to launch the bureaucratic innovation we need."

**Why it matters.** The June episode was, in her framing, a "pickup game" — improvised between a lab and a government with no shared facts and no standing process, which is how you get 90-minute takedown ultimatums. Her sharpest point: cyber was only the *first* threshold-buster; bio-capability is next, and there the sharing mechanisms don't exist at all. Read this against the Future of Life Institute's new [Summer 2026 AI Safety Index](https://futureoflife.org/ai-safety-index-summer-2026/) — nine labs graded by an independent expert panel, nobody above C+, with safety commitments *weakening* as capabilities accelerate. The voluntary regime is eroding before any institutional replacement exists; Hersman is describing what the replacement's first room looks like.

*Sources: Hersman, War on the Rocks (Jul 15); FLI Safety Index (~Jul 7 — grades from search-verified summaries). ~18 min.*

## ── RAPID FIRE ────────────────────────────────

**The Bad Apple problem** — Byrne Hobart reframes the Apple-OpenAI suit as organizational design: "Apple basically designed an organization that would generate as many valuable secrets as possible, which, unfortunately, created an incentive to steal them" — and rule-breakers cluster, so one damning team is weak evidence about a whole culture. Meanwhile the PR war escalated: leaked emails suggest Apple's counsel mixed up two employees' addresses, undercutting the "failed to respond" claim ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/07/15/openai-blames-email-mixup-for-why-it-didnt-respond-to-apple-trade-theft-claims) is properly skeptical of the convenient leak). (Byrne Hobart, [The Diff](https://www.thediff.co/archive/the-bad-apple-problem/), Jul 13)

**Anthropic found a "workspace" inside the model** — new interpretability work identifies a set of representations the model can verbalize and reason over — dubbed "J-space" — with an uncomfortable resemblance to global-workspace theories of human conscious access. Possibly the interpretability result of the year. (Anthropic, [Transformer Circuits](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html); Zvi's [walkthrough](https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2026/07/07/no-space-like-j-space/), Jul 7)

**Noise becomes the computer** — thermodynamic computing flips thermal noise from enemy to substrate: startup chips doing generative AI at claimed ~10,000× less energy, and a Berkeley Lab simulation reconstructing a face from static at ~100 billion times less heat than a digital neural net. The most credible third path through the AI energy crunch. (Philip Ball, [Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/thermodynamic-computers-go-with-the-energy-flow-20260715/), Jul 15)

**The minerals chokepoint moved** — for rare earths, gallium, lithium and graphite, the supply-chain bottleneck "is no longer at the mine, or even at the refinery. It is in the intellectual property" governing processing — concentrated in a handful of mostly Chinese firms. Contrarian and consequential for every AI-hardware supply argument. (Arezki & Rota-Graziosi, Nature, [via 3QD](https://3quarksdaily.com/3quarksdaily/2026/07/how-to-widen-access-to-the-critical-minerals-that-the-world-needs.html), Jul 15)

**Measuring AI search like a Super Bowl ad** — only ~1% of users click AI citations and 70% of AI-referred traffic lands as "Direct," so pipeline-from-clicks is structurally broken: "that's like valuing a Super Bowl ad by QR-code scans… The click isn't coming back. The demand already did." The most concrete AEO measurement framework yet. (Kevin Indig, [Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/how-to-measure-the-impact-of-ai-search-the-right-way), Jul 15)

**Russia returns to mass** — Kofman and Whisler on Russian force expansion: quantity is back as a central variable of the war, which changes NATO force-sizing math and every European rearmament timeline built on quality-over-numbers assumptions. (Whisler & Kofman, [War on the Rocks](https://warontherocks.com/a-return-to-mass-russian-force-expansion-in-the-war-with-ukraine/), Jul 14)

**Two types of talent** — Farnam Street's weekly: "Natural talent is overrated. Chosen talent is underrated" — plus the yes/no asymmetry worth taping to a monitor: "Every yes quietly steals time from the most important thing you're already working on." (Shane Parrish, [Brain Food #689](https://fs.blog/brain-food/july-12-2026/), Jul 12)

**⏳ TIMELESS: Heat Death — venture capital in the 1980s** — Jerry Neumann's definitive history of how overfunding, not the absence of winners, quietly destroyed a decade of VC returns after money quintupled into the same few hot sectors. Why now: banks just printed record equities revenue, SK Hynix is up 173% YTD, and this week's Money Stuff features an alleged *cocaine-linked SPAC targeting AI data centers*. The danger isn't a crash; it's 1983. (Jerry Neumann, [Reaction Wheel](https://reactionwheel.net/2015/01/80s-vc.html), 2015)

**🃏 WILDCARD: Foucault foresaw the authorless text** — his 1969 lecture "What Is an Author?" imagined discourse circulating "without any need" of an author — a spec sheet, 57 years early, for LLM-generated text and faceless algorithmic power alike. (Emily Eakin, NYT, [via 3 Quarks Daily](https://3quarksdaily.com/3quarksdaily/2026/07/from-a-i-to-the-deep-state-michel-foucault-foresaw-it-all.html), Jul 14)
