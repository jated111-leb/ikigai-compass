# The Daily Compass #001 — The week they tried to steer

*Sunday, July 12, 2026 · ~18 minute read*

Something shifted this week: the people who spent three years arguing about whether transformative AI is coming started arguing about whether anyone can steer it. A grand-strategy blueprint from the AI 2027 team, a million-line codebase rewritten by 64 parallel Claude agents, and a slow-motion portrait of work itself dissolving — today's issue is about steering: nations, companies, and careers.

---

## ── THE BRIEFS ────────────────────────────────

### ◆ 1. The AI 2027 team publishes "Plan A" — and everyone has an opinion

**What happened.** The AI Futures Project — Daniel Kokotajlo, Eli Lifland, Ryan Greenblatt and colleagues, the team behind the scenario document *AI 2027* — released their positive vision: a year-by-year roadmap to 2040 ([ai-2040.com](https://ai-2040.com/)) for navigating an intelligence explosion without catastrophe or techno-oligarchy. The linchpin: a US-China deal to slow superintelligence development, with joint control of chip supply, universal datacenter auditing, "mutually assured compute destruction," research transparency, and a Citizen's Dividend. Scott Alexander [introduced it Thursday](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/introducing-plan-a); Zvi Mowshowitz published [an exhaustive tour of the reactions](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/introduction-for-and-reactions-to) Saturday.

**The excerpt** (Zvi):

> "To be clear up front: I am not endorsing Plan A. I am not suggesting we should go off and try to enact Plan A as written. There is a lot more work to do and a lot of potential problems and downsides to grapple with. I do think we should do that work, and give it, and its details, serious consideration. … The central problem is either humans can collectively steer the future, or they can't."

**Why it matters.** Read this against two things. First, the ground truth: eleven days ago the US [lifted its export restrictions](https://fortune.com/2026/07/01/anthropic-fable-mythos-ai-models-restored-trump-administration-export-controls/) on Anthropic's most powerful models after two weeks of Washington negotiation — frontier AI is already being governed ad hoc, deal by deal. Second, the realist frame: Colin Kahl's January Foreign Affairs essay ["The Myth of the AI Race"](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/myth-ai-race) argued the most plausible endgame is "an asymmetric form of AI bipolarity" — America dominating frontier services while China wires good-enough AI into the world's physical economy — in which case Plan A's China-deal premise is fighting the current, not riding it. The debate's real value is that it converts doom discourse into something falsifiable. Whether you find the plan naive or necessary, the objections it forces into the open — *America would never; China would never; who audits the auditors* — are the actual agenda of the next decade.

*Sources: AI Futures Project; Zvi Mowshowitz, Don't Worry About the Vase (Jul 11); Scott Alexander, ACX (Jul 9). ~25 min for Zvi's tour.*

### ◆ 2. Spolsky's law just got broken: Bun was rewritten by 64 Claudes

**What happened.** Jarred Sumner [rewrote the entire Bun JavaScript runtime](https://bun.com/blog/bun-in-rust) — 535,000 lines of Zig — in Rust, using ~64 parallel Claude agents running for 11 days: 6,502 commits, a +1,009,272-line diff, verified against a language-independent test suite with ~1.4 million assertions and gated by "adversarial review" agents whose only job was to find why the diff was wrong. The result fixed 128 known bugs, closed every instrumentable memory leak, and shipped ~20% smaller binaries. It's been running inside Claude Code for a month; almost nobody noticed. (Disclosure that matters: Anthropic acquired Bun in December 2025, and Sumner used a pre-release Claude Fable 5.)

**The excerpt:**

> "Pre-merge, this took 5.9 billion uncached input tokens, 690 million output tokens, and 72 billion cached input token reads — around $165,000 at API pricing. By hand, I think this would've taken 3 engineers with full context on the codebase about a year… We never would've done that. The realistic alternative was to do nothing and keep fixing the bugs at the top of this post forever. This is the bleeding edge of what's possible today."

**Why it matters.** For twenty-six years, software strategy had one commandment, from Joel Spolsky's April 2000 essay ["Things You Should Never Do"](https://www.joelonsoftware.com/2000/04/06/things-you-should-never-do-part-i/): the ground-up rewrite is "the single worst strategic mistake that any software company can make," because old code embodies hard-won knowledge and rewrites freeze you while competitors move. Bun is the first prominent, apparently successful violation — and the mechanism is instructive: the conformance suite carried the hard-won knowledge, so the rewrite became a *verification* problem rather than a *memory* problem. When a doctrine that old flips, every "we can't afford to migrate" assumption on every roadmap — including yours — deserves re-pricing. The unresolved question is Armin Ronacher's (see Rapid Fire): what does it mean that only the lab that owns the harness can do this most cheaply?

*Sources: Jarred Sumner, Bun blog (Jul 8); Joel Spolsky (2000). ~20 min together — read them as a pair.*

### ◆ 3. The Big Drift: what if AI doesn't take your job, just the reasons for it?

**What happened.** The New Atlantis's summer issue runs ["The Big Drift"](https://www.thenewatlantis.com/publications/the-big-drift-an-ai-scenario), a phased scenario in which AI erodes work not through mass layoffs but through vanishing entry-level rungs, quietly recalibrated org charts, fiscal strain on wage-funded states, and UBI-cushioned lives that are "not in crisis, but not flourishing."

**The excerpt:**

> "In the coming years, artificial intelligence will not simply change how we work. It will likely erode work itself as the central organizing principle of modern society. This is not a claim about mass unemployment or human obsolescence. It is about what happens when work gradually ceases to perform the quiet functions it has long served: occupying large populations, structuring daily life, anchoring identity, and stabilizing social and political institutions."

**Why it matters.** The drift is already legible at the micro scale. This week developer educator Salma Alam-Naylor [quit the field entirely](https://whitep4nth3r.com/blog/goodbye-forever-probably/), citing Josh Comeau's data point that his new course is selling a third as many copies as a typical launch — learners now get personalized tutoring from the models themselves. An entire knowledge-economy niche, dissolving without a single layoff announcement. The individual counter-playbook is Benjamin Todd's evergreen 80,000 Hours guide ["How Not to Lose Your Job to AI"](https://80000hours.org/ai/guide/skills-ai-makes-valuable/): "while AI drives down the value of skills it *can* do, it drives up the value of skills it *can't* — wages (on average) will increase before they fall." The strategic move isn't fleeing the wave; it's repositioning toward the bottleneck skills — deploying AI on messy real problems, judgment, leadership — while the repricing is still early.

*Sources: The New Atlantis (Summer 2026); Salma Alam-Naylor (Jul 3); Benjamin Todd, 80,000 Hours. ~25 min for all three.*

## ── RAPID FIRE ────────────────────────────────

**Ben Thompson writes Zuckerberg's dream earnings speech** — a mea culpa for platform obsession and a mechanism for AI capex discipline: rent Meta's spare compute on the spot market so rental prices become an internal hurdle rate. "Our best product decisions have been intuition validated by data and revealed preference; that's how we're going to approach AI." (Ben Thompson, [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2026/a-script-for-mark-zuckerberg/), Jul 7)

**GPT-5.6 lands — and so does benchmark politics** — OpenAI's Luna/Terra/Sol family claims a 13-point lead over Claude Fable 5 on "Agents' Last Exam" while Fable 5 crushes it on SWE-Bench Pro (80% vs 64.6%) — a benchmark OpenAI just published an audit criticizing. The frontier duopoly is now openly fighting over which yardsticks are real. (Simon Willison, [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/9/gpt-5-6/), Jul 9)

**Tencent open-sources a frontier-adjacent agentic model** — Hy3: 295B-parameter Apache-2.0 MoE (21B active), 256K context, free on OpenRouter until July 21. China's open-weights strategy keeps compounding while America's flagships stay closed. ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hy3), Jul 6)

**ChinaTalk's 20 best findings of 2026 so far** — China holds ~2.7-2.8M H100-equivalents (~1/8 of global AI compute); Chinese AI leaders privately give only ~20% odds a Chinese model leads in 3-5 years; and Chinese firms buy Claude tokens at 90% off through Singaporean entities, then train on the outputs. Inoculates against both "export controls failed" and "export controls won." (Lily Ottinger, [ChinaTalk](https://www.chinatalk.media/p/the-20-most-interesting-ideas-weve), Jul 7)

**"Stupidity is not the opposite of intelligence but its evil twin"** — the Santa Fe Institute's David Krakauer argues stupidity requires sophisticated cognition — and scales with it — ending with AI hallucination as the opening act of "super-stupidity." The sharpest epistemic framing of AI risk this month. (David Krakauer, [Nautilus](https://nautil.us/what-makes-humans-stupid-1282459), Jul 8)

**Better models, worse tools** — Flask creator Armin Ronacher documents newer Claude models inventing JSON keys on third-party edit tools that older models handled fine — likely RL overfitting to Claude Code's native harness. "Tool schemas are not neutral… the more post-training happens inside one dominant harness, the more every other harness will have to inherit its quirks." (Armin Ronacher, [lucumr.pocoo.org](https://lucumr.pocoo.org/2026/7/4/better-models-worse-tools/), Jul 4)

**How Amsterdam invented the fire department** — Jan van der Heyden's 17th-century tech (suction hoses, air-chamber pumps) cut fire losses by 99% — but only because he redesigned the institutions around it: district companies, incentive-laced alarms, fines and rewards. The eternal lesson for AI deployment: tools pay off when the system is rebuilt around them. (Virginia Postrel, [Works in Progress](https://worksinprogress.co/issue/how-amsterdam-invented-the-fire-department/), Jul 1)

**⏳ TIMELESS: Untangling Skill and Luck** — Mauboussin's paradox of skill: as absolute skill rises and converges, *luck matters more* to relative outcomes. Surfaced today because every AI-era success now being canonized — Nvidia's position, Meta's "prescient" 2022 GPU buy, DeepSeek's rise — is a skill/luck attribution problem, and this is the canonical toolkit. (Michael Mauboussin, [HBR](https://hbr.org/2011/02/untangling-skill-and-luck), 2011)

**🃏 WILDCARD: Harari at 3 Quarks Daily** — the site that inspired this newsletter curated a Harari talk arguing AI has "hacked the operating system of human civilization" — language, and everything that runs on it: law, money, religion. ([3 Quarks Daily](https://3quarksdaily.com/3quarksdaily/2026/07/yuval-noah-harari-ai-has-hacked-the-code-of-human-civilization.html), Jul 3)

## ── THE QUEUE ─────────────────────────────────

**Invest Like the Best — Jeremy Giffon: "The Billion Dollar PDF"** (Jul 7, ~2h likely)
Giffon distills 18 months of conversations with founders and allocators into one thesis: attention has replaced capital as the scarce resource, and AI's per-inference cost ends software's zero-marginal-cost era — razor-thin margins at massive scale become the only survival path. **Queue it** — the most idea-dense strategy episode of the window; the software-margins argument alone justifies the run time. ([Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/episode/23weyKcIhpXMVteLeMW5ZA))

**Conversations with Tyler — Joel Mokyr on Clans, Corporations, and a Culture of Growth** (~Jul 8)
The freshly minted Nobel laureate on why sustained growth became normal: propositional knowledge plus a culture that tolerates disruption — and why Europe organizing cooperation among *non-kin* (guilds, universities) while China organized around the clan mattered for a thousand years. **Queue it** — Cowen is at his best with economic historians, and this is core operating-system knowledge for thinking about AI-era growth. ([conversationswithtyler.com](https://conversationswithtyler.com/episodes/joel-mokyr/))

**Hard Fork — AI consciousness, with NYU's Jeff Sebo** (Jul 10)
Roose and Newton on social-media age bans, then Sebo on whether AI systems could become conscious and what "AI welfare" research even means. **Queue the Sebo segment** — the most accessible treatment yet of a question moving from fringe to lab policy; pair it with Noema's essay by a neuroscientist-turned-AI-researcher, ["When the Machines Deserve Our Consideration"](https://www.noemamag.com/when-the-machines-deserve-our-consideration/). ([Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/hard-fork/id1528594034))
