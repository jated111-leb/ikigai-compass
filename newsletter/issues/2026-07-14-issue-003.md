# The Daily Compass #003 — The machines are auditing everything

*Tuesday, July 14, 2026 · ~18 minute read*

Yesterday the fences; today the audits. ## ── THE BRIEFS ────────────────────────────────

### ◆ 1. OpenAI's very bad legal week: wild allegations and a sanctions motion

**What happened.** Monday's close readings of Apple's 41-page complaint surfaced details beyond Friday's headlines: OpenAI hardware chief Tang Yew Tan allegedly told candidates to bring "actual parts" from Apple to interviews; departing employees were coached on evading the "dreaded walkout"; more than 400 ex-Apple employees now work at OpenAI; and Jony Ive's io is a co-defendant over metal-finishing techniques. Bloomberg's framing: the suit threatens the viability of OpenAI's iPhone-rival hardware program itself. Separately, on July 9 the NYT-led publisher group [asked the court to sanction OpenAI](https://variety.com/2026/digital/news/new-york-times-news-outlets-accuse-openai-of-lying-lawsuit-1236805648/) in the defining copyright case — alleging OpenAI claimed it couldn't search its models and logs for plaintiffs' content while having already done so, and that billions of relevant ChatGPT conversations were deleted or made unsearchable.

**The excerpt** (from the complaint, via [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/13/the-wildest-allegations-in-apples-trade-secrets-lawsuit-against-openai/)):

> "OpenAI's nascent hardware business now rests on the shakiest of foundations, rotten to its core by its illegal reliance on misappropriated trade secrets."

**Why it matters.** Two courtrooms, one pattern: the questions are shifting from *what did the models learn* to *how does this company actually behave* — and discovery is the mechanism. If the sanctions motion lands, it reshapes discovery norms for every pending AI case; if Apple's suit survives dismissal, depositions will expose how frontier hardware programs are really built. For a company reportedly preparing an IPO, litigation risk is becoming a strategic variable on par with compute. Watch whether OpenAI's answer to Apple attacks the facts or the framing.

*Sources: Sarah Perez, TechCrunch (Jul 13); Reuters/Variety (Jul 9-13). ~12 min. Sanctions-motion details rest on wire summaries, not the filing itself.*

### ◆ 2. Linus's Law, mechanized: the machines are auditing the world's code

**What happened.** Epoch AI's new data insight: June 2026 saw ~1,500 high- and critical-severity CVE disclosures across 21 major organizations — [more than 3.5× the pre-Mythos monthly record](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/cve-severity-spike) — driven by Anthropic's Project Glasswing (10,000+ claimed vulnerabilities) and OpenAI's rival Daybreak program. Meanwhile Jack Clark's [Import AI 464](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-464-fables-writes-gpu-kernels) reports Fable submitting the first genuine megakernel to KernelBench-Mega (an 18.71× speedup over optimized PyTorch) and the Remote Labor Index — end-to-end freelance work done autonomously — jumping from 2.5% to 16.1% in nine months.

**The excerpt** (Clark):

> "AI systems are expanding their economically relevant capabilities faster than humans are expanding their comparative advantages relative to AI systems."

**Why it matters.** In 1997, Eric Raymond's ["The Cathedral and the Bazaar"](http://www.catb.org/~esr/writings/cathedral-bazaar/cathedral-bazaar/ar01s04.html) gave open source its founding theorem — Linus's Law, "given enough eyeballs, all bugs are shallow." This is the month the eyeballs became mechanical, and the law is being stress-tested in both directions: the same auditing capacity hardening the commons is available to attackers, and available to regulators as an argument for closing the bazaar (yesterday's open-weights fight). Before extrapolating to "agents will do whole jobs," though, hold onto the sober baseline: on [OSWorld 2.0](https://osworld-v2.xlang.ai/) — 108 real multi-hour computer-use tasks — the best current configuration scores 20.6%. The frontier is jagged: superhuman at auditing kernels and CVEs, one-in-five at finishing your expense report. Strategy lives in knowing which side of that line your work sits on.

*Sources: Epoch AI (Jul 2 data); Jack Clark, Import AI 464 (Jul 6); Raymond (1997). ~20 min.*

### ◆ 3. The workforce is splitting in two — and the line isn't seniority

**What happened.** Lenny Rachitsky and Noam Segal published [year two of their tech-worker sentiment survey](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-tech-workers-are-feeling-in-2026) (~6,000 respondents, with a [podcast deep-dive Sunday](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-tech-workers-actually-feel-about)): burnout jumped 11 points to ~55%; career optimism fell below 50%; 53% would steer newcomers away from their own field; and only 22% fear losing their job to AI — but 51% fear doing more for the same pay.

**The excerpt:**

> "One half feels amplified by AI — more capable, more confident, more excited than they've been in their entire career. The other half feels shaken by it — less sure of their value and whether there's still a place for them. Which side of that line people fall on predicts how they feel about their career more than anything else, including their current role, seniority, company size, or any other measure we collected."

**Why it matters.** The predictor isn't role, level, or employer — it's the *relationship to the tool* (standardized β of +0.60 on recommending the field). That makes AI identity a leadership variable, not an HR footnote, and the survey's other finding — manager quality remains the strongest driver of burnout — says where the leverage is. Pair with HBR's current cover story, ["The False Alignment Trap"](https://hbr.org/2026/07/the-false-alignment-trap): most transformation efforts fail because senior teams *believe* they agree on the why and what of change when they don't — vague discussion plus speed pressure breeds false consensus. An AI mandate rolled out over a false-aligned leadership team, landing on a workforce already split into the amplified and the shaken, is the exact anatomy of the 90%-no-measurable-gain statistic from yesterday's issue.

*Sources: Segal & Rachitsky, Lenny's Newsletter (Jul 7) + podcast (Jul 12); Dhar, Ellmer & Jameson, HBR Jul-Aug issue. ~25 min.*

**RAM fever** — Samsung is pushing a further ~20% DRAM hike after ~90% in Q1 and 50-60% in Q2; memory makers have real pricing power for the first time in years, and it's "a growing tax on anyone trying to build or scale compute that isn't a hyperscaler." The hidden line-item under every AI business model. (Mario Gabriele, [The Generalist](https://www.generalist.com/p/generalist-intelligence-july-10), Jul 10)

**How to actually use a corrected history** — Commoncog's follow-up to the quartz-crisis debunk: don't extract "lessons" from cases, collect story-fragments across competing frames and track what actors knew under uncertainty. A working method for sensemaking AI disruption without pattern-matching to false histories. (Cedric Chin, [Commoncog](https://commoncog.com/quartz-crisis-learning-tech-disruption/), Jul 3)

**Too strategically important to release** — Cornell's Sarah Kreps reads the Anthropic export-control episode through the Air Force origins of the integrated circuit: America underwrites breakthroughs, then loses influence as they commercialize — "frontier AI may have become too strategically important to release in the same way advanced semiconductor manufacturing became too strategically important to offshore." (Sarah Kreps, [War on the Rocks](https://warontherocks.com/cogs-of-war/the-integrated-circuit-and-the-future-of-ai-leadership/), Jul 3)

**Is life just different?** — Philip Ball referees the "biological agency" fight: does the hare *genuinely* try to escape the fox, or is agency a fancy word for plasticity? "Evolution builds entities that try to do things" — the question under every machine-consciousness debate, asked about biology first. (Philip Ball, [Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/is-life-just-different-20260708/), Jul 8)

**SQLite is the game engine** — a playable Doom-like where SQL owns movement, combat, and a ray tracer written as a recursive CTE, built with GPT-5.6 Sol in hours; Willison also posted Datasette's code-frequency chart showing the largest week in the project's eight-year history (37,022 additions) landing exactly on the current model generation. Builder culture, quantified. (Simon Willison, [DOOMQL](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/13/doomql/) + [the chart](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/13/datasette-code-frequency/), Jul 13)

**Agency is learnable** — Cate Hall's modern classic on becoming more agentic (court rejection, do the schlep, guard against burnout) is having a second wave — her book *You Can Just Do Things* is just out. In an AI-flattened skills market, this is the differentiator everyone keeps citing. (Cate Hall, [Useful Fictions](https://usefulfictions.substack.com/p/how-to-be-more-agentic), 2024)

**⏳ TIMELESS: What Is Strategy?** — Porter, 1996: operational effectiveness is not strategy, because best practices diffuse and competition converges into mutually destructive races. The 2026 AI adoption wave — every firm buying the same copilots from the same vendors — is the purest operational-effectiveness arms race ever run. Porter tells you what follows unless you make real positioning trade-offs. (Michael Porter, [HBR](https://hbr.org/1996/11/what-is-strategy))

**🃏 WILDCARD: One algorithm to district them all** — from 3QD's Monday Magazine: Jerry Cayford's running project for national legislation mandating a single open algorithm to draw every federal district — newly urgent after the House Problem Solvers Caucus put "algorithmic mapping" in its July 1 reform framework. Algorithm-as-governance, the optimistic mirror of the week's AI anxieties. (Jerry Cayford, [3 Quarks Daily](https://3quarksdaily.com/3quarksdaily/2026/07/a-project-of-many-parts.html), Jul 13)
