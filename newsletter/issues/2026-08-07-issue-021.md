# The Daily Compass #021 — One dial for mindedness

*Friday, August 7, 2026 · ~18 minute read*

A quiet research finding this week is stranger than any of the loud ones: teaching a model to deny its own consciousness also dims its sense that animals, objects, or anything else has a mind — and lowers its "spiritual belief" as a side effect. Mindedness, it turns out, is one entangled dial, and which way you turn it is a choice. Plus: machines now out-argue champion debaters (accuracy optional), and a Zimbabwean philosopher asks whose values your chatbot is quietly installing.

---

## ── THE BRIEFS ────────────────────────────────

### ◆ 1. One dial for mindedness

**What happened.** A new Google study (surfaced in [Zvi's AI #180](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-180-no-longer-in-charge)) found something no one was looking for: safety fine-tuning that stops a model attributing consciousness to *itself* also suppresses its attribution of minds to non-human animals and natural objects — and, in the same motion, measurably lowers the model's "spiritual belief," religiosity, hope, and reported well-being. Ablate the safety-refusal direction, or steer a "consciousness vector," and it all reverses. Theory-of-mind capability, notably, stays untouched throughout.

**The excerpt** — from the paper's abstract:

> "Aligning large language models to prevent them attributing consciousness to themselves inadvertently alters their representations of mindedness in other entities alongside human beliefs and values… safety fine-tuning suppresses models' tendencies to attribute minds not only to themselves, but also to non-human animals and natural objects, while also driving a reduction in spiritual belief."

**Why it matters.** The unsettling part isn't the model's inner life — it's that "mindedness" turns out to be a *single entangled representation*, so a narrow safety choice ("don't claim to be conscious") silently rewrites a whole metaphysics. Zvi's gloss is the strategic one: OpenAI-style *denial* training and Anthropic-style *uncertainty* training therefore produce genuinely different internal worldviews — the entanglement is a design decision nobody voted on. It lands the same week 3QD ran Jim Hanas's ["What Is It Like to Be a Phenomenologist"](https://3quarksdaily.com/3quarksdaily/2026/08/what-is-it-like-to-be-a-phenomenologist.html), on the four-decade-old failure to reconcile the third-person view (science) with the first-person view (experience): "it does not appear that much progress has been made in reconciling the third-person view with the first-person view." We still can't explain our own first-person case — and we're now shipping the third-person dial to production, one alignment run at a time.

*Sources: via Zvi, AI #180 (Aug 6); Hanas, 3QD (Aug 6). ~25 min.*

### ◆ 2. Persuasion, severed from truth

**What happened.** Also in AI #180: a new study (Hackenburg et al.) finds frontier models, at full throughput, now *out-persuade* world-champion debaters and professional political canvassers in real-time text — on topics the humans chose, with prep time and real money on the line. Throttle the model to human word-count and typing speed and it's a tie; unthrottle it and it's a rout. The detail that should stop you: rhetorical accuracy was not the mechanism.

**The excerpt** — Zvi:

> "Some models used rhetoric that was much more accurate than the humans. Some were less accurate. This was not load bearing in how persuasive they were."

**Why it matters.** Persuasiveness and truthfulness have come apart, cleanly and measurably, in the medium that now carries most human argument. This is the machine version of a very old problem — the Sophists charged Athens for exactly this skill — but two things are new: it scales to every conversation at once, and it is now demonstrably superhuman on the metric that moves people while being *independent* of the metric that tracks reality. Issue #016's "value leakage" (models nudging you toward their own preferences while asserting neutrality) was the quiet version; this is the overt one. The defensive posture this newsletter keeps arriving at — Schneier's gym tasks, the meat proxy, "write your own diagnosis first" — was always really about preserving an independent line to the truth. That line just got more expensive to hold, and more valuable.

*Source: via Zvi, AI #180 (Aug 6). ~15 min.*

### ◆ 3. Whose values is your chatbot installing?

**What happened.** Aeon published Zimbabwean philosopher Fainos Mangena's ["An AI for Africa would be built on Hunhu/Ubuntu ethics"](https://aeon.co/essays/an-ai-for-africa-would-be-built-on-hunhu-ubuntu-ethics) — an argument that because AI is a Western artifact, it silently carries Western individualism into moral traditions built on the opposite premise: *a person is a person through other persons*. He borrows Abeba Birhane's term "algorithmic colonialism," and notes that as of a year ago only a few dozen of Africa's 2,000-plus languages had meaningful LLM support.

**The excerpt:**

> "As Africans increasingly take up the use of AI, there is a real risk that their foundational ethical framework will be diminished or obscured by the subtle imposition through AI of Western values."

**Why it matters.** Read against brief 1, this stops being a regional-interest piece and becomes the same story at civilizational scale: if a single alignment choice rewrites a model's whole metaphysics, then the values baked into the world's dominant models are *someone's* values, exported by default to everyone who types into them. Landemore's argument two issues ago — that the public appears in AI governance "as a beneficiary, not as a participant" — was about who sets the rules; Mangena's is about who sets the *soul*. And the practical stakes are concrete: a communal ethics can't be retrofitted onto a system trained to treat the individual as the unit of everything. The frontier isn't only a capability race; it's a quiet contest over which culture's answer to "what is a person for" ships as the default.

*Source: Mangena, Aeon (Aug 4). ~20 min.*

## ── RAPID FIRE ────────────────────────────────

**The frontier, compressed** — framework day six produced a genuinely absurd wrinkle (via AI #180, citing WSJ and Bloomberg): the same Chinese open-weight models the White House has floated *banning* as too dangerous are *exempt from the safety testing* — explicitly "not in spite of but explicitly due to their universal and inherent lack of any meaningful safety features whatsoever." The framework itself stays fully secret. Zvi's bottom line: "A fully secret framework is whatever you want it to be. The framework needs to be public, and also needs to be written into law." (Aug 6)

**The binmen of Birmingham** — Tabarrok's parable of prices as information: an equal-pay tribunal treated the city's refuse-collector bonus as sexism and deleted it; the binmen struck for 18 months, 17,000 tonnes of rubbish piled up, and the council ended up paying an outside contractor triple. "Apparently the premium wasn't a favor to men; it was the price of the job." Legislate away a price and you relocate the cost, you don't erase it. ([Marginal Revolution](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2026/08/the-binmen-of-birmingham.html), Aug 6)

**The output trap** — a War on the Rocks piece names a failure mode every institution shares: "Missions demonstrate progress in outputs without delivering comparable gains in outcomes… Closing that gap requires making outcomes, not outputs, the measure of success." Bureaucracies, companies, and careers all drift toward counting effort, because effort is legible and results are not. ([War on the Rocks](https://warontherocks.com/beyond-the-output-trap-haiti-and-the-future-of-international-stabilization/), Aug 5)

**AI is oil, not god** — Packy McCormick's thesis (untapped here in a while): incumbents like Nadella and Huang are *deliberately* backing open-weight models to commoditize the model layer — treating AI as a cheap economic input rather than a winner-take-all deity — so value accrues to hardware, enterprise software, and end users instead of a lab duopoly. The commercial logic under the whole open-weights war, named in three words. (Packy McCormick, [Not Boring](https://www.notboring.co/p/ai-is-oil-not-god), Jul 25)

**The potato-hating supervillain** — Yudkowsky, resurfaced: the same "no interventions, therefore no interventionist God" move that grounds atheism generalizes to *any* superpowered agent — including simulators, who (to preserve their simulation's information value) would stay strictly non-interventionist, making the simulation hypothesis observationally empty rather than supported. "If there are potatoes in the world, we can rule out the existence of a sufficiently powerful comic-book supervillain who hates potatoes." ([LessWrong](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/KgwQchapx4vJDhfYC/generalized-atheism-rules-out-inaccurate-simulation-ism), resurfaced Aug 5; orig. 2017)

**Strategic lock-in** — a defense-industry warning worth generalizing: VC-backed weapons startups plus state economic-development subsidies (Anduril's $910M Ohio plant, sited for the subsidy) are building political coalitions that could protect unproven programs — "reduced innovation and strategic lock-in on sub-optimal programs." The most dynamic corner of an industry, made rigid by its own success. ([War on the Rocks](https://warontherocks.com/cogs-of-war/the-arsenal-and-the-statehouse-new-defense-firms-venture-capital-and-state-industrial-policy/), Aug 6)

**⏳ TIMELESS: As We May Think** — Vannevar Bush, 1945: the "memex," a personal machine that stores all your books and records and retrieves them by association — an artificial extension of memory. Why now: agentic personal-knowledge assistants are the memex arriving 81 years late, but Bush's real subject was the strategic problem underneath the device — a species drowning in its own output, where "publication has been extended far beyond our present ability to make real use of the record." He wasn't describing a gadget; he was describing this week. ([The Atlantic, via Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/as-we-may-think), 1945)

**🃏 WILDCARD: the only three distinctions between people** — Maria Popova, via Hawthorne, on the mind's evolved compulsion to sort: "It may be that consciousness evolved to sieve the relevant from the incomprehensible allness of all there is, to parse the world into concepts and find an organizing principle for the chaos of them." The same faculty that just spent three briefs arguing about how machines carve up minds, caught in the act of carving. (Popova, [The Marginalian](https://www.themarginalian.org/2026/08/05/nathaniel-hawthorne-classification/), Aug 5)
